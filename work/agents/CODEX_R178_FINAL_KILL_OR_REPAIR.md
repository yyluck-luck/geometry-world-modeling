## Decision (ranked)

**1. Retain conditionally as a falsifiable benchmark hypothesis (current decision).** The narrow mechanism remains conceptually distinguishable only when a purported unique certificate is evaluated against hidden provenance/label counter-worlds. R176 did not establish novelty; R177 shows the current protocol is not acceptance-ready.

**2. Do not promote to a benchmark claim yet.** Generic abstention and label-ambiguity baselines may subsume the task, and the proposed counter-world can be non-identifiable or leak through certificate formatting.

**3. Retire if the blockers below cannot be satisfied without exposing the latent world or changing observable semantics.** This is the explicit fallback rather than an open-ended refinement loop.

## Exact acceptance blockers

1. **World specification:** preregister two latent worlds and answer sets independently of model outputs; define uniqueness and ambiguity without circular reference to the requested rejection label.
2. **Leakage proof:** canonicalize IDs, ordering, whitespace, confidence, and templates; a metadata-only classifier must remain at chance within a preregistered margin.
3. **Semantic control:** byte identity after canonicalization must be paired with blinded independent semantic/geometric review confirming that only the declared hidden convention differs.
4. **Matched controls:** include known-unique items with identical certificate surface formatting and a generic abstention/label-error baseline with frozen prompts.
5. **Identifiability:** independent annotators must agree on admissible interpretations and counter-world non-equivalence; otherwise discard the item family.

The packet must include at least one proposed item, one known-unique control, and one leakage-audit result or an explicit missing-evidence marker. Its purpose is to test implementability and identifiability before any benchmark execution.
