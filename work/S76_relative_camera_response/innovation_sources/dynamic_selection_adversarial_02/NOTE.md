# Switching dynamics: a classical falsifier of covariance-only selection

**A regime-aware memory idea must beat ordinary change-point filtering; detecting a change and discounting stale observations is already established.** This source check identifies a baseline, not a validated video method. It is separate from S76 camera-response results, which were not accessed.

**Verified primary method.** Adams and MacKay, *Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection*, arXiv:0710.3742v1, 19 October 2007, derives an online posterior over time since the last change. Prediction averages over run-length hypotheses; conjugate models retain per-run sufficient statistics. Its model assumes independent segment parameters and i.i.d. observations within each segment. Exact per-step memory/computation grows with observed length; the paper describes tail truncation but retains a linear worst case. Therefore change-aware sufficient-statistic memory is not new, and bounded cost must be declared. [Author-hosted paper, §§1–2.4, Algorithm 1](https://lips.cs.princeton.edu/pdfs/adams2007changepoint.pdf)

**Foundational multiple-model boundary, only partly verified.** Blom and Bar-Shalom, *The Interacting Multiple Model Algorithm for Systems with Markovian Switching Coefficients*, IEEE TAC 33(8):780–783, 1988, describes approximate Bayesian filtering through hypothesis merging for switching linear systems. The author-upload record/abstract and DOI were identified; full-text routes failed. This batch does not certify exact IMM recursions, a current implementation, or any frame-selection result. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/9.1299) · [author-upload record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3020085_The_interacting_multiple_model_algorithm_for_systems_with_Markovian_switching_coefficients)

**Counterexample (symbolic, no experiment).** Let observations be y_t=mu_t+epsilon_t, independent epsilon_t~N(0,sigma²), with mu_t=0 before a change and mu_t=d afterwards. The future target mean is d. Under a misspecified single constant-mean model with a flat prior:

- Every k-observation subset has posterior variance sigma²/k, independent of the observed values.
- An all-pre-change subset predicts mean 0 in expectation; an all-post-change subset predicts d.
- Their expected squared errors for the future mean are d²+sigma²/k and sigma²/k, respectively.
- Thus the covariance objective ties while prediction quality differs by d². Same camera coverage and observation count cannot repair the omitted regime variable.

**Strong fair baseline to name:** a BOCPD-informed recent-regime selector, or a finite switching-model filter when the regime family is known. For a k-frame consumer, ranking eligible history by current-run posterior membership and retaining temporal spread is a conventional derived selector, not an algorithm claimed by the BOCPD paper. Freeze the ranking/tie rule, hazard/noise model, finite hypothesis budget, and any fit data before evaluation. A filter consuming all history is an auxiliary competitor unless the proposed selector has the same feature access and pays the same preprocessing/filtering cost; do not give only one consumer an extra state summary. Keep k, generator, actual noise, denoiser calls, and all annotations equal.

**Falsifier:** if a calibrated change-aware baseline matches the proposed event-memory policy, reject the claim that a distinct event-allocation mechanism is required. Beating a deliberately static Gaussian model is insufficient. A new regime absent from every candidate model, an outlier mistaken for a change, or a transition completely hidden during occlusion remains a real limitation. Change-point detection cannot reveal an unobserved event; such cases require uncertainty, not invented recovery. No new protocol, threshold, code, model run, or novelty claim is introduced.
