**Three task contracts must stay separate.**

| Contract | Observable eligibility rule and permitted interpretation |
|---|---|
| Short continuation without new contact | Before the cutoff, the allowed frames show the object's motion and no current hand/tool attachment; timestamps and camera motion support a predeclared constant-velocity or ballistic approximation. This only makes it provisionally eligible. A separate auditor must later check the entire fixed interval for new contact, occluded attachment, support change or other intervention. No current contact does not guarantee no future contact; gravity/friction still exist. |
| Future action supplied | A timestamped control plan relevant to object dynamics must be available to the predictor before its forecast and actually consumed, with the same plan supplied to comparisons. A target camera path alone does not qualify. Hand trajectories extracted from future RGB are privileged outcome information, not naturally available commands; such a diagnostic changes the task. Even a supplied action need not specify every force or hidden property. |
| Unknown future human action | Keep a conditional distribution over possible futures. History may reveal intention, so missing actions do not prove independence or irreducible randomness. One observed outcome cannot identify that distribution. Several cameras recording the same event are not independent action repetitions. A sample's MSE is descriptive, not a memory-defect diagnosis or probability-calibration test. |

The ordinary marginalization identity makes the confound explicit: p(Y|H,C) = ∫p(Y|H,C,U)p(U|H,C)dU, where U is the unprovided future manipulation, H the allowed history and C the supplied camera/query information. Changing history can alter beliefs about U as well as estimated object state. A changed RGB score alone cannot separate these paths. This is standard probability, not a proposed algorithm.

**Smallest useful next step, not executed here.** After the separately authorized data preparation, inspect one predetermined short two-view window with a fixed cutoff and horizon, using the declared history budget. Record prefix evidence for object identity, attachment/contact and motion. Freeze an elementary trajectory forecast before the future audit. Keep every prefix-eligible window in the record; mark unexpected interventions or unverifiable contact as such rather than shortening the horizon or silently replacing the case. The separate future view is for auditing and scoring, never the predictor. This requires a bounded annotation pass and elementary arithmetic, no neural model or full-scene reconstruction.

If a valid uninterrupted interval exists, the conditional question is whether same-budget retained observations preserve enough motion information for that limited forecast. Start with recent/uniform and ordinary motion prediction. If the interval contains unknown manipulation, or the contact audit is inconclusive, stop attributing its error to memory. If an ordinary baseline already solves the witness, no new selector is justified. Distributional forecasting would instead require multiple suitable cases and a predeclared event-level proper score, such as Brier score, with all samples retained; one coffee-making realization cannot supply that validation.
