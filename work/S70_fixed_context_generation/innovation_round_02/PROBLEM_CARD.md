**Decision.** Pursue the information question below as an alternative to normalization. The literature contains a relevant public failure, but this bounded search did **not** verify the stronger witness “correct geometry AND the required references demonstrably consumed, yet dynamics failed.” Keep that conjunction unproven. Do not turn reference availability into a causal explanation.

**One concrete reported failure.** Ma, Liufu and Gkioxari, *Out of Sight, Out of Mind? Evaluating State Evolution in Video World Models* ([arXiv:2603.13215v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13215), submitted 13 March 2026), §4.2/Fig.7, PDF p.8, reports VMem recalling the initial scene while a ball fails to roll. Crucially, §4.1, pp.5–6, evaluates VMem only qualitatively because of camera-pan artifacts; it is absent from Table2. §3.2 evaluates evolution after control success, but that protocol does not certify exact 3D geometry or per-source cache consumption. The paper is verified as a **preprint**, not a formally accepted conference paper. Its architecture/data explanations remain hypotheses. Thus this is evidence for a specific appearance-recall/dynamics distinction, not proof of a pure consumer failure with all geometry held correct. [Original PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13215)

**Why the strict witness matters.** SPMem's formal NeurIPS2025 Fig.6/§5 failure explicitly involves reconstruction artifacts and TSDF filtering away valid points; it fails our “correct geometry” premise. GEN3C's §6 limitation says dynamic generation requires a pre-generated motion video, so correct static geometry alone does not specify dynamics. WorldStereo supplies pointmaps and paired references; its component scores do not establish this particular state test. FramePack and Self Forcing already address memory/drift and rollout distribution, with limits retained in S59. These are reused constraints, not newly invented mechanisms. [SPMem](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05284v1) · [GEN3C](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03751v1)

**One transferable principle.** Vafa et al., *What Has a Foundation Model Found? Using Inductive Bias to Probe for World Models*, [ICML2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/vafa25a.html), §2.1–2.2, distinguishes respecting equal states from distinguishing different states. §4 tests a coarser legal-next-move explanation. Reusing the saved official PDF, the transferable idea is to construct inputs on which two proposed state descriptions predict different outcomes. Their actual method uses many adapted tasks and oracle calibration; our source-level test is not their inductive-bias metric or its replication.

**Conditional question.** Does the selected reference bundle retain enough temporal information to distinguish the dynamic states demanded by a revisit task, before we ask whether the generator uses that information correctly?

An elementary example makes this testable. In an ideal constant-velocity world, two sharp observations show a ball at x=0 then x=1 from the same calibrated camera. If their times are 0 and1, one time unit after the latter observation the ball is at2. If the times are0 and2, it is at1.5. The image pair, geometry, order, and target camera can be identical; the observation times differ. This is a mathematical counterexample to sufficiency of untimed snapshots, not a VMem experiment or a claim that natural images always match exactly.

The bound original pipeline already exposes a relevant boundary: lines1249–1259 retrieve `context_time_indices`; lines1261–1284 pass latents, embeddings, cameras, K and mask through `get_cond` to `do_sample`; the time indices instead go to reconstruction at1307. Neither the explicit acquisition intervals nor a requested physical elapsed time is supplied there. With identical complete generation inputs and random state, a deterministic execution must agree; more generally its conditional output law is identical. It cannot distinguish the two toy timing cases through a missing input. Slot order or learned fixed-frame-rate conventions may carry implicit timing, so this is **not** evidence of failure on the model's declared fixed-step task. It rejects silently extending that task to arbitrary elapsed-time state prediction.

| Competing explanation | Discriminating prediction and stop rule |
|---|---|
| Required temporal state is absent from the consumer input | Different required futures collapse to the same complete input. Stop calling this a learned-memory or geometry-use failure; the proposed task contract is insufficient. |
| Temporal state is present but ignored or overwritten | Motion/timing-disambiguated inputs remain distinct, yet later outputs behave like last-state copying. This requires a real dynamic witness, valid camera execution and actual consumed references; it has not been demonstrated here. |
| Ordinary visibility/reconstruction or data-domain failure | The purported reference is missing, misregistered, stale, or dynamics already fail under full observation. Stop claiming the interruption caused a specific persistent-state failure. |

**Smallest local diagnostic and what is missing.** The source trace above is a zero-model sufficiency screen and is already completed. Before any new video run, select one real dynamic track with at least two timestamped observations, a known continuation across an interruption, and camera/visibility evidence; establish whether its distinguishing timing actually reaches the consumer. Existing TUM metadata certifies camera motion and sensor references, but does not certify such a paired evolving-object example or uninterrupted independent state reference. No broad pixel scan was performed; this is a missing verified witness, not a claim that every TUM frame is static. A static nine-frame turn cannot distinguish recall from evolution because copying is compatible with its task.

For a future valid dynamic witness, ordinary last-state persistence and constant-velocity extrapolation are the first transparent baselines. The latter's timing/state annotations must be declared as extra information where the model lacks them. No oracle result should be called a fair model improvement. The cheap next decision is therefore data/input sufficiency, not another 50-step run. No new algorithm, arms, data download, or training is authorized by this card.

**Nearest collision.** Memory Forcing's preprint §3.3/Eq.3 already mixes temporal and spatial context by exploration/revisit regime; §4.4 ablates training and retrieval. Its geometry is reconstructed, so it does not supply our strict witness either. Timestamping, a static/dynamic split, a temporal-spatial gate, or correcting the input contract would be ordinary engineering unless a distinct mechanism and real benefit survive stronger controls.
