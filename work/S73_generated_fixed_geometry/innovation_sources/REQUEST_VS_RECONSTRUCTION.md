# S73: request-versus-output pose evidence and one inexpensive falsifying control

**Decision:** S73 can establish supported fixed-request inconsistency, not a unique camera cause. A reconstructed output trajectory would add a distinct measurement, but it remains an estimator output and first needs real-input qualification. The most economical next falsifying control is a fixed wrong-pose-label comparison on the existing real correspondences, with no new generator or descriptor. `NO_METHOD_SELECTED`.

**New primary-source detail.** CameraCtrl: Enabling Camera Control for Video Diffusion Models (Hao He, Yinghao Xu, Yuwei Guo, Gordon Wetzstein, Bo Dai, Hongsheng Li, Ceyuan Yang), author v2 of13March2025, compares COLMAP-recovered poses against input camera conditions. AppendixD.5 sets first frames to identity, rescales generated translations using the first-two-frame GT/reconstruction displacement ratio, and excludes failed COLMAP clips. AppendixE warns of instability even with14/16-frame videos. Crucially, AppendixF.3 runs COLMAP on real16-frame RealEstate10K clips and compares with their GT to estimate an empirical error floor. Thus real-input estimator qualification is an explicit close precedent; its reported floor is not transferable to our TUM five-view/approximate-K setting. [CameraCtrl author text, D.5/E/F.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.02101v2)

**Can poor reconstruction explain a high fixedF error?** Distinguish two meanings. Merely substituting another *consistent static3D shape*, rendered under the same cameras with correct same-point correspondences, still satisfies epipolar geometry; wrong depth alone is not a reason for high residual. But view-dependent deformation, inconsistent objects/background across views, or generated content that no longer represents the real anchor's points destroys the shared-point/static-scene premise. Weak/repetitive texture can also produce wrong matches or leave only a biased subset. These can produce high residual without uniquely identifying a camera change. A separate SfM result can itself fail or fit an alternative scene/pose compromise. This is geometric reasoning, not a finding about which failure occurred in S70.

**One proposed control, not executed:** keep each real19→realj pair's entire existing matched coordinates and cameraK unchanged. Compare its correct requestedF against exactly one predeclared target-pose derangement:20↔23 and21↔22. Construct the wrongF from camera19 and the permuted target pose under the same optical convention; do not refitF, alter intrinsics, rematch, sample pixels, tune a threshold or search permutations. This is an intentional wrong-request control on identical observed support, not a new calibration model or realistic generated negative.

For each target, report all paired residual differences `e_wrong−e_correct`, its median, counts and coverage. The fixed descriptive prediction is a positive median difference for every target. Any nonpositive target falsifies this *all-four selectivity prediction*; missing/degenerate geometry makes it unknown, never a dropped target. If this wrong label is equally compatible, the observer has insufficient discrimination for this control, possibly because the poses have similar epipolar geometry; do not rescue it by choosing a different permutation. A positive result only shows sensitivity to this wrong-label alternative, not uniqueness of pose or sufficient sensitivity to all errors. No significance claim or new cutoff is introduced.

**What to defer:** a CameraCtrl-style reconstructed-pose comparison should retain the real anchor, expose recovered/failed camera counts and supported points, and first measure the same estimator on the real anchor+four real targets with the sameK/gauge policy. A real-control failure or instability blocks strong generated-camera interpretation. The current bounded review has not supplied or run that estimator. Do not use an unconstrained reconstruction or post-hoc camera refinement to certify the originally supplied pose.

Ordinary estimator calibration, pose-label negative controls and availability-aware reporting are baseline diagnostics. They may rule out an interpretation; they do not establish a novel mechanism or resolve the source of the observed generated framing failure.
