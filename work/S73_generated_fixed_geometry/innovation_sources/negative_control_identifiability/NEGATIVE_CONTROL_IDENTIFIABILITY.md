# When a wrong pose label is not a distinguishable negative

**Conclusion:** the fixed20↔23,21↔22 derangement tests only discrimination between those pose labels on the observed real correspondences. Similar epipolar geometry can make it uninformative even with perfect images, correct calibration and exact correspondences. Conversely, all-positive wrong-minus-correct residual differences do not establish calibrated cameras, statistical significance or the cause of S70's generated framing failure. The earlier control proposal remains a conditional sensitivity check.

**One exact symbolic condition.** Let all optical camera rotations be identity, all intrinsics be the same invertibleK, anchor center be0, and target centers be `c_j=a_j d`, for fixed nonzero directiond and nonzero scalarsa_j. This describes distinct positions along one line, including different traveled distances. The relative world-to-camera translation is `t_j=−a_j d`, hence

`F_j = −a_j K^(−T)[d]×K^(−1)`.

Swappingj for any other targetk gives `F_k=(a_k/a_j)F_j`. The symmetric point-to-line residual used by S72/S73 is invariant to any nonzero scalar multiplyingF: numerator and line norm scale by the same absolute value. Therefore every measured pair has exactly the same residual under correct and wrong labels, regardless of depth variation or match quality, wherever the line distances are defined. Frobenius-normalized matrices coincide up to sign. The negative control cannot identify translation magnitude along this common direction. This is a symbolic proof, not a statement that our saved trajectory satisfies these assumptions.

**Nearby cases and planarity.** With small rotation changes and nearly parallel translation directions, normalizedFs can be close, so the paired residual difference can be small where line normalization is well-conditioned. Near-zero line normals require the existing invalid-geometry handling; this continuity argument is not a universal bound near epipoles. Planarity alone does not guarantee that the selected wrongF will fit equally well: it matters whether the supported points constrain the competing camera hypotheses differently. A dominant plane or restricted image region can reduce discriminating evidence, and many points on that same support do not create independent geometric information. The exact collinear example already applies to planar **and** nonplanar scenes; do not infer this failure solely from an apparent wall or floor.

**One preregisterable reporting requirement: disclose the control's geometric separation.** For each of the four fixed label swaps, before reading its residual comparison, report

`separation_j = min(||F̂_j−F̂_swap(j)||_F, ||F̂_j+F̂_swap(j)||_F)`,

where `F̂=F/||F||_F` andK/crop conventions stay fixed. Pair this value with the fixed label IDs, full match count/coverage and the already-proposed paired residual summary. The statistic is dimensionless in this fixed image-coordinate convention; it is not a pose-error or pixel-error metric. Preserve the existing zero-baseline/invalid-line cases, and do not choose a new pose permutation because separation or results look weak. No post-hoc “sufficient separation” cutoff is proposed: show the continuous value; exact zero is the analytic non-identifiability case. Any finite-precision equality rule must be fixed before actual calculation rather than inferred from the result.

If wrong and correctFs are indistinguishable, a nonpositive/zero residual difference is an expected lack of control separation, **not evidence of a faulty observer**. If they differ and all four paired medians are positive, the permissible conclusion is only that this observer discriminated these alternatives on these real matched points. Four targets share one anchor and one short scene; four positive signs are not four independent trials and cannot be converted into an unregisteredbinomial significance claim. Neither result verifies the distortion model, absolute translation, reconstruction completeness or all other camera alternatives.

**Closest-source interpretation.** CameraCtrl D.5 explicitly removes a translation-scale degree of freedom in its reconstructed-pose metric, and F.3 measures its estimator's error on real videos. Those are useful precedents for stating which degrees of freedom are observable and for measuring an empirical error floor; neither constitutes a transfer guarantee for this TUM observer. The already-read VMem/SEVA comparison likewise separates requested numerical poses from a reconstructed or correspondence-derived assessment. Even successful real-negative discrimination cannot explain high generated residual uniquely. Changed correspondence identity, view-dependent geometry, appearance mismatch, timing/calibration and camera behavior remain distinct possibilities. The proposed control tests real-observer sensitivity, not a repair or diagnosis of S70's generator. `NO_METHOD_SELECTED`.
