# Fixed-k RGB history selection: closest collisions

**Verdict: NO_METHOD_SELECTED.** No exact equivalent to every element of the proposed experiment was established in this three-paper bounded read. That is not positive novelty evidence. The broader claims of selecting useful historical frames, recovering older event evidence, and query-dependent allocation are already occupied.

Contract being tested: choose k original past RGB observations for a known target object/task; preserve older persistent-state evidence despite later unrelated events; keep the generation consumer and denoiser budget unchanged. Encoded representations of individually selected frames count as frame selection; arbitrary token pooling or captions are different interfaces. Known task/camera requests are allowed input if shared; future pixels, answers and outcome-derived selection labels are not.

## What could actually defeat the proposed contribution?

First use the ordinary overlap-plus-recency-plus-redundancy selector, keeping the current consumer fixed. Then add a simple object-identity state tracker that preserves the latest **informative observation of the queried object**, not the latest event anywhere. If that ordinary rule matches a proposed belief selector at k frames and the same available features, there is no demonstrated selection mechanism beyond established retrieval/state estimation. A caption-query ranker is another collision baseline only if its preprocessing and information access are accounted for; capping VideoTree and feeding RGB to our consumer would be an explicitly modified baseline, not a reproduction of its reported protocol.

A narrower unresolved empirical question is whether earlier diagnostic evidence is necessary when newer same-object observations are visually compatible with multiple persistent states, and whether selecting it helps this unchanged consumer. An identity/state baseline may already solve it. If full informative history also fails, the selector has not been isolated as the bottleneck. A successful frozen comparison would establish a conditional information-allocation effect; it would not by itself establish a novel algorithm.

No paper here proves equality to the complete proposed contract, and no theorem or attention map substitutes for that equivalence. Conversely, “unchanged consumer” and “fixed k” are experimental controls, not sufficient novelty by themselves. Nothing in this note authorizes a new experiment, use of future outcomes for selection, or changes to shared protocols.
