**Logical direction matters.** Let the joint hypothesis include correct correspondences, a shared static scene, requested relative pose, correct timing and the stated imaging approximation. With fixed requested `F`, sufficiently large residuals under a qualified observer contradict this joint hypothesis; they do not uniquely identify camera error. Wrong matching, moving/deformed objects, identity changes, calibration/distortion or timing can also violate it. This is a conditional geometric rejection, not a registered statistical significance test. Low residual merely fails to reject: it is neither proof of correct camera nor a rejection by itself.

A symbolic counterexample needs no payload: for `K=I`, `R=I`, `t=(b,0,0)`, nonzero b, the epipolar equation reduces to `v'=v`. Arbitrary horizontal disparity can satisfy it. Thus incorrect depth, scale or along-line displacement may remain invisible. Zero baseline makesF degenerate; weak texture, restricted coverage or a dominant plane can leave a low residual uninformative. These are mathematical limitations, not measurements of our frames.

The S71 pose-metric note's fixed real-history anchor remains appropriate: freezing requested geometry avoids fitting away the tested camera relationship, and a real→real control exposes the combined observer/input error before generated content is involved. All four targets and matching failures must remain. A later fr2-calibration sensitivity analysis must specify the complete K/distortion and inverse crop mapping in advance; merely swappingK in the old distorted coordinates is not a calibrated test. It cannot alter the K actually consumed by S70 or establish that calibration caused its failure.

**One closest recent primary method.** *Epipolar Geometry Improves Video Generation Models*, arXiv2510.21615v1, §3.1–3.3, uses SIFT correspondences, normalized eight-point/RANSAC estimation ofF, and Sampson error to form preferences for Flow-DPO; it adds a temporal-variation penalty against static solutions. Its static-scene training choice explicitly avoids independently moving objects corrupting geometry. §4.5 reports coverage-related misleading low errors and low-error artifacts; AppendixC retains the dynamic-object limitation. These are the authors' method and reported caveats, not results reproduced here. [Author preprint, method and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21615v1)

**Exact distinction and innovation verdict.** That fitted-F score asks whether generated pairs admit a plausible projective relation. Our fixed requested-F check instead tests compatibility with a declared request and a real historical anchor. This distinction improves interpretability, but is standard two-view geometry and does not by itself constitute a new evaluation mechanism. Likewise, replacing approximateK, adding distortion handling, switching descriptors, checking spatial coverage or using epipolar residual as a reward is already ordinary practice or directly covered by the closest method. No presently specified mechanism survives as a justified novelty claim. The remaining useful question is empirical: after observer qualification, do generated correspondences violate the fixed request beyond what real pairs do? Even a positive finding would locate joint inconsistency, not uniquely its cause. `NO_METHOD_SELECTED`; no new model or dataset expansion is proposed.
