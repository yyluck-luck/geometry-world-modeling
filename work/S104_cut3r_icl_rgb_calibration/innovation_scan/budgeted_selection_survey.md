# Budgeted Subset-Selection Mechanisms (Cross-Domain) — Survey for the GRC / geometry-aware world-model project

Scope: transfer survey. For each mechanism family: (a) selection objective + guarantee, (b) cost model / how "budget" is formalised, (c) whether it has already been applied to video-generation memory or 3D memory caches, (d) trivial-rename vs genuine mismatch.

**Method note.** Every URL below was fetched in this session. Where I could not fetch a primary source, I write UNVERIFIED. "The paper claims X" and "X is true" are kept distinct. Pre-2020 mechanisms are reported only where a recent paper ports them into a relevant setting.

## 2. GENUINELY NOT YET PORTED

These are the mechanisms where I found no fetched evidence of a video-generation or 3D-memory application, and where the mismatch is real rather than cosmetic.

1. **Risk-averse / CVaR submodular maximisation** (Zhou & Tokekar, https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.09358).
   *Why it transfers non-trivially:* every selection mechanism I found in the video/3D-memory literature — including GIM-World's §3.4 and H₂O's dynamic-submodular formulation — optimises the **expected** informativeness of the retained set. GRC's premise is different in kind: it wants to avoid the tail event where the retained memory is *confidently wrong* (a bad surfel that poisons the target region). CVaR submodular maximisation is precisely the machinery for "optimise the worst-case-ish quantile rather than the mean", it has an existing approximation guarantee under a **matroid** constraint, and the guarantee's dependence on function **curvature** gives a principled way to report when the risk-aware objective is and is not worth it. I found **no** video-generation or 3D-cache paper doing this (UNVERIFIED, but searches surfaced no such work).

2. **Adaptive submodular / EC² selection with non-uniform, correlated-noise test costs** (Golovin, Krause & Ray, https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.3091).
   *Why it transfers non-trivially:* this is the only family I found whose **cost model natively handles heterogeneous costs and correlated noise**. GRC's budget is stated as "fixed number of memory slots / fixed FLOPs" — i.e. items have *different* costs (a distant keyframe is cheaper to attend to than a dense surfel block) and the reliability scores are noisy and spatially correlated. Every video-side mechanism I fetched (DensityKV, OmniMem, PaFu-KV, GIM-World) uses a **uniform per-item cardinality budget**. Porting a non-uniform-cost, adaptive objective is a real modelling change, not a rename. Note the mechanism is from 2010, but it is interesting here specifically because no recent video paper has ported it.

3. **Explicit geometric-reliability term coupled to a *future*-error objective** (no single canonical paper — this is the gap).
   *Why it transfers non-trivially:* GIM-World's criterion is `I(S ; H\S)` — informativeness *about the frames it drops*, i.e. a retrospective coverage objective. GRC wants `argmin_S E[future prediction error | S]`, which is a **different functional**: it is not symmetric in the dropped set and it is not monotone in coverage, because a geometrically unreliable item can *increase* future error even while increasing coverage. If the project can show that the coverage-optimal subset is not future-error-optimal — a concrete, falsifiable claim — that is a genuine and defensible delta over GIM-World. Absent that demonstration, this collapses into family 1 above.

Secondary candidates (real mismatch, but weaker/less differentiated):
- **Coreset k-center geometry** (Sener & Savarese, https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.00489). The k-center objective is pure *coverage* of the pose/feature manifold, so on its face it is a re-derivation of GIM-World's GP coverage criterion. It becomes non-trivial only if the "distance" is replaced with a geometry-*error* metric (e.g. reprojection residual under the surfel cache), at which point it is really family 1/3 again.
- **CDVM-style constrained data-value maximisation** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11312). Interesting because it *penalises excessive per-test contribution*, i.e. it explicitly trades total influence against redundancy — structurally close to "don't let one memory item dominate the target region". But its objective is training-set influence, and the paper's own framing is data pruning for model training, so porting it requires re-deriving influence for a *generative* future-loss, which is nontrivial but also unfalsifiable cheaply.

## 3. TRIVIAL / ALREADY DONE

These would be read as a rename or a straightforward composition. Do not lead with them.

1. **Plain greedy coverage maximisation with a fixed cardinality budget** — already rejected by the project, and correctly so: GIM-World (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02436, §3.4) *is* this, applied to geometry-memory selection in a video world model, and it cites the Krause sensor-placement criterion by name. Proposing it again is a direct scoop.
2. **Information-gain / expected-information NBV applied to 3D memory** — FisherRF + RaEM (https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11396) already does expected-information-gain view selection on NeRF/3DGS, and already layers a risk measure on top. A `GRC = FisherRF-for-memory` framing is a composition of two published ideas.
3. **Attention/activation-magnitude KV eviction** — H₂O (https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14048) and StreamingLLM (https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453) are the canonical LLM versions, and the video-side variants (DensityKV https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27922, OmniMem https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30519, PaFu-KV https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21896) have already been published for autoregressive video diffusion. Porting "score items by attention, keep top-k" is standard.
4. **Redundancy/density-based deduplication of stored state** — DensityKV's Soft-Riesz neighbourhood-density criterion (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27922) is exactly this, for a historical KV bank at a fixed capacity. It is also close to the project's already-rejected "simple keyframe deduplication".
5. **Retrieval-augmented history with a learned query embedding** — LongLive-RAG (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02553) and Context-as-Memory (DOI 10.1145/3757377.3763833) cover content-addressable historical-latent retrieval. A camera-pose-keyed version of this is a small delta.
6. **Learned salience scoring for cache retention** — PaFu-KV (https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21896) distils a salience head for exactly this purpose in autoregressive video diffusion. "GRC scores each memory item" is the same architecture with a different label unless the score is a *geometric reliability* quantity, not a salience quantity.
7. **Test-time gradient-descent memory writing** — GradMem (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13875) already does loss-driven test-time memory optimisation at fixed memory size. Available as a transfer, but it is a different axis (how to *write* memory) than GRC's (what to *keep*).

**Bottom line for the project:** the "score memory items → pick a fixed-budget subset → reduce future error" recipe is **not novel as a shape**. The published delta must be (i) the score is a *geometric reliability/risk* quantity rather than a coverage or salience quantity, and (ii) the objective is *future prediction error* rather than retrospective informativeness or reconstruction. Those two must be shown to matter empirically.

## 4. Top 3 transfer candidates — instantiation + cheapest falsifier

### (a) CVaR-optimised memory selection (risk-averse submodular max)
**Instantiation.** Replace GIM-World's `max_S I(S ; H\S)` with a CVaR objective over the *reliability-weighted* utility of the retained set. Concretely: let each memory item `i` (surfel block or keyframe) carry a scalar `u_i(region)` = expected reduction in future prediction error contributed in the target region, and let `ξ_i` be a random variable encoding the geometric unreliability of item `i` (e.g. multi-view photometric residual, depth-consistency spread, or VGGT-feature disagreement). Define a submodular `f(S)` = coverage of the target region by retained items, then maximise `CVaR_α[f(S) − λ·Σ_{i∈S} ξ_i]` under the fixed-budget matroid. Solve with Zhou–Tokekar's Sequential Greedy, reporting the curvature-dependent guarantee as a sanity check on whether the risk term is doing anything.
**Cheapest falsifier.** Do not build the full pipeline. Take a held-out short sequence with ground-truth future frames, take the existing memory cache, and compute two subsets of equal size: (1) coverage-greedy (the project's rejected baseline / GIM-World's criterion) and (2) CVaR-sequential-greedy. Render both and measure future-frame error. **Falsified if the CVaR subset is not better on the error tail** — specifically, if the 90th-percentile future error over target regions is not lower. If the tail is no better, the risk machinery is decoration and the mechanism should be dropped. This needs no retraining, only subset selection + evaluation.

### (b) Non-uniform-cost adaptive selection (EC² / adaptive submodularity)
**Instantiation.** Drop the uniform "fixed number of memory slots" fiction. Give each candidate item a measured cost (bytes of surfel storage, or attention FLOPs to condition on it) and each query a *sequence* of acquisition decisions: at step `t`, the geometry estimator reports its current belief and the selector picks the next item maximising expected reduction in future prediction error per unit cost, updating beliefs after each pick. EC²'s guarantee applies if the objective is adaptive monotone and adaptive submodular, which is plausible here because the marginal informativeness of a memory item diminishes as more of the target region is already explained. Report the ratio to the uniform-budget baseline at *matched FLOPs*, not matched item count.
**Cheapest falsifier.** On a single scene, sweep `k` for the uniform-budget selector and plot future prediction error vs. FLOPs. Then plot the adaptive selector's curve on the same axes at matched FLOPs. **Falsified if the two curves coincide within noise** — that would mean item costs are effectively uniform in this setting and the adaptive machinery buys nothing. This is a pure measurement on existing caches; it needs no new model.

### (c) Explicit reliability term against a future-error objective (the actual GRC delta)
**Instantiation.** Make the objective *future-error reduction* rather than coverage, and make the score *geometric unreliability*, then show the two are not the same ranking. Formally: for each candidate item `i`, estimate `Δ_i = E[future error without i] − E[future error with i]` in the target region via a cheap surrogate (one-step denoising loss on held-out future latents, or reprojection error of the surfel block into the target view), and estimate `ρ_i` = probability the item is geometrically unreliable in the target region. Retain the budget-`k` subset maximising `Σ_{i∈S} Δ_i` penalised by `ρ_i`, subject to a *diversity* term so the solution is not degenerate. The claim to test is that `argmax Δ` ≠ `argmax coverage`.
**Cheapest falsifier — and the most important experiment in this survey.** Take the existing cache and, for a set of target views, compute (1) the coverage-greedy subset and (2) the `Δ`-greedy subset at equal size. Report the **rank correlation between the coverage score and the `Δ` score across items**. **Falsified immediately if the correlation is high (say Spearman ρ ≳ 0.8)** — that means coverage is a good proxy for future-error reduction, GIM-World's criterion already captures GRC's intent, and the project's contribution reduces to a rename. Conversely, a *low* correlation, plus a demonstration that the coverage-optimal subset contains items with high `ρ` in the target region, is the single cleanest piece of evidence that GRC is a real mechanism. Run this before anything else.

## 6. Caveats

- The classical `(1-1/e)` greedy bound (Nemhauser–Wolsey 1978) and the JMLR Krause–Singh–Guestrin full text were **not fetched** in this session; treat those two as UNVERIFIED. The GIM-World HTML page I fetched attributes its criterion to "Krause et al." and states the greedy algorithm is used, which is verified.
- H₂O's abstract states a guarantee exists "under mild assumptions" but does not state the bound; I did not fetch the theorem. UNVERIFIED.
- I was unable to fetch a primary URL for BOCPD (Adams & MacKay 2007). Every claim about BOCPD in this document is UNVERIFIED. The project should treat "BOCPD for invalidating stale geometric memory" as an open, unclaimed direction rather than a covered one — but it must verify the BOCPD primary source before citing it.
- All arXiv IDs of the form 26xx.xxxxx are from 2026 and were fetched successfully this session. Several are very recent (DensityKV, Aug 2026) and may be preprints without venue acceptance; I have not verified their peer-review status. Their *claims* are reported as claims.
- I did not exhaustively search for a paper that combines CVaR/risk-averse submodular selection with video or 3D memory. The absence of such work is based on the searches listed above, not on a proof of non-existence.
