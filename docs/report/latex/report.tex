\small
\textbf{Question.} A released view-generation system selects four historical frames to condition
each generation. On a fixed panel it delivers only three \emph{distinct} frames into those four
slots in 10 of 14 windows, because two independently seeded selections return the same frame. Does
that wasted slot cost image quality?


\smallskip
\textbf{Intervention.} Replace the redundant slot with the pipeline's own next distinct ranked
candidate, in place, changing nothing else. Retention threshold $+0.20\dB$, fixed in writing before
any repair output was scored.


\smallskip
\textbf{Result.} $\mathbf{-0.016\dB}$, across the 8 of 10 duplication-affected windows in which the
prespecified policy offers a replacement candidate. The no-op control passed byte-identically.


\smallskip
\textbf{Decision.} The performance explanation is discarded and this branch is closed. No further
generation is planned.
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.8em}

\section{Outcome and stopping decision}

A prospectively specified intervention was run and did not meet its criterion.

The released selector delivers only three distinct frames into four context slots in 10 of 14
evaluated windows. The proposed explanation was that this wasted slot materially costs image
quality. The test was an in-place replacement of the redundant slot with the pipeline's own next
distinct ranked candidate, with a retention threshold of $+0.20\dB$ fixed in writing before any
repair output was scored.

Measured: $-0.016\dB$ in mean across the 8 of 10 duplication-affected windows in which the
prespecified policy offers a replacement candidate (SD across windows $0.309$, 3 of 8 positive). In
the remaining 2 affected windows the intervention could not be instantiated under the prespecified
candidate-selection policy, because that policy provided no fourth distinct candidate: requesting up
to seven entries still returned four, holding three distinct frames (record in
Appendix~\ref{app:excluded}). That is a property of the prespecified read policy, not a
demonstration that those windows are untestable under any possible design. The no-op control
passed: all 8 window--seed executions in the 4 unaffected windows reproduced the sealed output
byte-identically.

The duplication-as-performance-explanation is \textbf{discarded}. The threshold was not lowered, the
replacement policy was not changed, and no favourable subgroup was elevated. \textbf{This branch is
closed.}

Failure to meet a $+0.20\dB$ criterion is \emph{not} proof of no effect. The source-level cause of
the duplicate selection remains established; only its proposed material PSNR consequence does not.
Two results were also withdrawn during the work and are recorded in Section~\ref{sec:corrections}.

\section{Scope and the quantity measured}

\textbf{Data.} Two exposed development sequences, RGB-D Scenes v2 \code{scene\_13} and
\code{scene\_14}, seq-01. These sequences are exposed: they shaped the hypotheses during this
project. Their target frames are excluded from conditioning, but the sequences are \emph{not}
independent held-out evaluation data and the term ``held-out'' is not used for them anywhere.

\textbf{Panel.} 14 fixed windows (7 window starts $\times$ 2 sequences; window start 0 fails
retrieval in both sequences and is excluded with that reason). Bank $=$ start $+\{0,5,\dots,55\}$,
12 frames. Targets $=$ start $+\{60,75,90,105\}$, i.e.\ forward extrapolation beyond the bank. Two
fixed seeds (42, 7). One dependency group. One frozen consumer.

\textbf{Unequal horizons.} The fixed-offset arm may use frames only up to offset $+45$; the
retrieval bank extends to $+55$. Retrieval therefore has a candidate-availability advantage. This is
\emph{not} a guarantee of any performance ordering: a frozen generator's output is not monotone in
candidate proximity, and this selector carries no dominance guarantee.

\textbf{Metric.} RGB PSNR only. Reference frames are loaded at native $480\times640$, converted to
float, area-resized to $576\times768$, cropped to columns $96{:}672$, and quantised to uint8.
Predictions are mapped from $[-1,1]$ to $[0,1]$ when needed and quantised identically. \textbf{MSE is
accumulated over all four targets of a window--seed before a single PSNR is taken.} No geometry
metric, no perceptual metric, no human study.

\textbf{Aggregation.} Seeds are controlled stochastic replications, not independent scenes. The two
seeds are averaged \emph{within a window first}; all reported statistics are then window-level.

\textbf{Inference.} Windows are a finite panel, not a sample from a population. The quantity measured
is the mean over exactly these windows and seeds. \textbf{No standard error, $t$ statistic,
confidence interval, significance test, equivalence test or bootstrap appears in this report.} SD is
reported solely as a description of heterogeneity across the panel.

\textbf{What is compared.} Every arm receives real historical frames. The principal baseline
comparison is between retrieval-selected historical context and fixed-offset historical context. The
remaining contrasts compare state initialisation or context-selection variants against one another.
\textbf{None of them is a comparison of memory versus no memory.}

\subsection{Validity checks, with their exact coverage}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Order-invariance gate} (job 595599, zero diffusion). The name refers to invariance of
the \emph{retrieval selection} to the order in which arms are executed. It says nothing about whether
generated output is invariant to context slot order, which is a different question addressed in
Section~\ref{sec:results}.2. The generation worker's own isolation routine makes the effective
threshold, the ordered frame ids and the padding multiplicities equal between ``clean arm alone'' and
``other arm first, then isolate, then clean arm''. 11/11 checks, including a non-vacuity assertion
that the test can detect the contaminated ordering it guards against. Covers 3 windows of
\code{scene\_13}.
\item \textbf{Disabled-branch threshold independence} (job 595625, zero diffusion): forcing
\code{initial\_threshold} to the primed percentile, \code{1e8}, \code{1e-9}, \code{0.0}, and deleting
it, leaves the NMS-disabled selection unchanged. 6/6 windows. Deleting does not raise, because the
disabled branch assigns before reading --- which is also why it is the leak source.
\item \textbf{NULL byte-identity gate}: 4 window--seed executions (2 NULL windows $\times$ 2 seeds).
All sha256-identical between the clean and leaked arms.
\item \textbf{No-op byte-identity gate}: 8 window--seed executions (4 unaffected windows $\times$ 2
seeds). All sha256-identical to the sealed shipped output.
\item \textbf{Seal verification}: all 28 new outputs and all 88 sealed outputs re-hashed and matched
before any target RGB was opened.
\item \textbf{Historical linkage}: the contaminated ordering was reproduced 28/28 byte-identically
against the sealed run, and the census independently reproduced the sealed leaked selections
frame-for-frame.
\end{itemize}

Each gate certifies exactly the cases it covers and nothing beyond them.

\section{Interpreting the failed repair}

Three distinct claims must be kept apart.

\textbf{The cause of three-distinct-frame delivery is established.} With NMS disabled the selection
is seeded with \code{sorted\_frames[0]} (the surfel-nearest candidate) and then
\code{len(self.c2ws) - 1} (the most recent stored frame). \textbf{In 10 of the 14 evaluated
forward-extrapolation windows those two coincide.} When it occurs, the duplicated index counts
toward the requested context length, so the fill step cannot recover the slot and only three
distinct frames reach four slots. Forward extrapolation is the setting in which this was observed;
it is \emph{not} shown to be a sufficient condition, since four of the fourteen
forward-extrapolation windows do not exhibit it. It is visible directly in the ranked indices:
$[11,11,10,9]$, index 11 being simultaneously the nearest candidate and the most recent frame.

\textbf{Whether repairing it improves PSNR was tested and the answer did not meet the criterion.}
$-0.016\dB$ over 8 of 10 affected windows.

\textbf{Whether repeated information actively harms generation was not tested and cannot be answered
here.} That would require an absent-slot control $(a,-,b,c)$ with a genuinely absent context slot and
everything else preserved. Under a four-frame selection-only intervention that leaves the
conditioning interface untouched, no such control exists: a blank image is still an input, any other
existing image changes multiplicity, and removing a slot changes sequence length and target
placement. No substitute was attempted and none is claimed.

\textbf{Why a near-zero mean is not a near-zero effect.} With mean $-0.016\dB$ and SD $0.309$ over 8
windows, per-window effects of a few tenths of a dB in both directions cancel. Similar PSNR values
also do not imply similar images: two outputs can have comparable error against the target while
differing from each other. Only the byte-identity gates establish exact sameness, and only for the
cases they cover.

\textbf{Why the interventions do not identify a distance-sensitivity mechanism.} All three figures
below are signed differences, not magnitudes. Reordering a multiset: $-0.015\dB$ (4 windows, clean
minus leaked). Replacing a duplicate with the next ranked nearby frame: $-0.016\dB$ (8 windows,
in-place minus movement-path). The NMS policy contrast: $-0.726\dB$ (14 windows, clean NMS-on minus
movement-path). Those three have different interventions, different comparison arms and different
window sets, and ``same multiset'', ``nearby frame'' and ``distant frame'' are not three levels of a
controlled variable --- a larger frame index separation does not establish a proportionally larger
change in pose, visible content, or the conditioning tensors. A competing explanation, that this is
simply a monotone response to larger input perturbation, is not excluded. \textbf{The defensible
statement is only that the tested NMS policy produced a larger negative mean contrast than the other
tested interventions, each on its own window set.}

\section{Relation to prior work, and limitations}

Read at primary source on 18 September 2026. Each of the following occupies a claim this project
might otherwise have made, and each is therefore recorded as a reason \emph{not} to claim novelty.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{VRAG} (\arxiv{2505.21996}v4): ``The History Buffer method performs poorly, with an SSIM
score of 0.188, indicating that naive historical frame retrieval without effective in-context
training fails to maintain long-term consistency.'' Occupies ``historical retrieval need not beat
recent-window conditioning''. Its own 300-frame table is not uniformly negative across metrics, so it
must not be cited as a blanket negative.
\item \textbf{Context as Memory} (\arxiv{2506.03141}): context-selection ablation, Random
$17.70/17.07$, FOV+Random $19.17/17.47$, FOV+Non-adj $20.11/18.19$. A published redundancy-selection
ablation in the same units used here.
\item \textbf{RAGME} (\arxiv{2504.06672}): named deduplication of near-duplicate retrieved videos in
retrieval-augmented video generation.
\item \textbf{LongLive-RAG} (\arxiv{2606.02553}): retrieval collapsing toward temporally local
neighbours, with a loss that suppresses redundant local similarity.
\item \textbf{COVRAG} (\arxiv{2606.02479}): finer geometric evidence with independent selection
(MEt3R $0.149$, LPIPS $0.210$) is worse than coarser evidence with independent selection
($0.141$, $0.198$); residual selection recovers it ($0.100$, $0.156$).
\end{itemize}

Two further sources, \textbf{PRoPE} (\arxiv{2507.10496}) on camera reference-frame dependence and
\textbf{TTAB} (\arxiv{2306.03536}, ICML 2023) on episodic versus accumulated evaluation state, closed
two directions that were considered and abandoned before the repair experiment. They are recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:closed}.

\textbf{Relation to VMem's own evaluation.} The paper describes non-maximum suppression as part of
its method: ``To avoid oversampling repeatedly visited regions, we apply a non-maximum suppression
algorithm that reduces redundancy in memory and promotes broader scene coverage among the top-$K$.''
Its memory demonstration is \S4.3, ``Long-term view generation with revisitations''; its $K=4$
selector ablation reports temporal selection at $7.52\dB$ against VMem at $14.82\dB$. \textbf{This
report's panel contains no revisitation: every target lies beyond the end of the conditioning bank.
This work therefore does not reproduce, test, or contradict that experiment, and no absolute score
from this panel is comparable with one from it.}

\textbf{Limitations.} One consumer cannot establish prevalence across a model family. One dependency
group cannot establish an average effect across environments. Exposed sequences cannot later serve as
untouched validation. Additional seeds, windows or replays would supply no independent scenes and
would not widen the metric's scope. PSNR contrasts establish RGB prediction-error differences under
the stated conditions and nothing about perceived realism, long-horizon scene consistency, or world
model usefulness.

\section{Corrections, disposition, and artifact index}
\label{sec:corrections}

\subsection{Corrections made during this work, all retained in the record}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A contaminated arm was interpreted as a memory result.} The sealed NMS-enabled arm ran
under a threshold inherited from a preceding NMS-disabled call. Its $-0.729\dB$ figure was withdrawn
as an estimate of the independently initialised effect and replaced by \code{memory\_nms\_on\_clean}
at $-0.485\dB$. The original number is retained as the result of a specified order-dependent
execution.
\item \textbf{A positive mean was described with the wrong sign.} The $+0.242\dB$ contrast was
written as ``does not beat fixed-offset context'' and ``a bounded negative result''. Both retracted.
\item \textbf{Inferential statistics were reported for a finite panel.} SE $0.340$ and $t$ $0.71$
withdrawn; SD retained as heterogeneity only.
\item \textbf{``Held-out'' was used for exposed sequences.} Narrowed throughout.
\item \textbf{The release's call graph was traced by parameter name rather than by function name.}
This produced four false claims --- that every released call disables NMS, that the config default is
unreachable, that NMS-on is not shipped behaviour, and that the leak belongs only to comparative
harnesses. All four are retracted; Section~3 gives the complete three-site call graph. The procedural
lesson is recorded in the project principles: a parameter-name search finds only the sites that
override it, and the count of overrides is never the count of call sites.
\item \textbf{A repair mapping mixed a content change with a position change.} The \code{refill} arm
was retained and reported, and the in-place arm was added and made primary, with the specification
fixed before scoring.
\item \textbf{A repair denominator was stated without its exclusions.} Corrected to ``8 of 10
duplication-affected windows'', with the 2 exclusions and their structural reason documented.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Disposition}

The research branch is closed. No further generation is planned. No novelty claim is supportable from
this work and none is made. \code{new\_method\_validated} remains \code{false};
\code{novelty\_authorization} remains \code{NONE}.

An open question to the upstream authors is prepared but \textbf{not sent}: whether the per-action
retrieval setting in \code{navigation.py} is intentional, and which entry point and configuration
produced the selector ablation. It is phrased as a question, scoped to the exact commit and files
inspected, carries no inference about intent, and does not attach this project's measurements.

\section{Addendum, 18 September 2026: post-hoc target-wise scoring note}

Added after the report was finalized. It does not alter the primary results, the prespecified scoring
rule, or the decision to close the duplicate-slot repair branch.

\textbf{Post-hoc target-wise analysis.} At target offsets $+60$, $+75$, $+90$, and $+105$, the mean
framewise PSNR contrasts for NMS-off minus fixed-offset context were $+2.950$, $+0.398$, $-0.180$,
and $-0.321\dB$, respectively, with seeds averaged within windows. Their arithmetic mean is
$+0.712\dB$. This uses a different aggregation from the prespecified four-frame pooled-MSE PSNR
contrast of $+0.242\dB$ and is not its additive decomposition. The profile is descriptive and does
not isolate temporal distance, target-slot position, or context clustering as a cause.

The two aggregations differ because the prespecified score pools MSE across the four targets before
taking one PSNR, whereas the framewise figures take one PSNR per target and average. The resulting
gap is non-negative and \textbf{arm-dependent} --- measured here as $+0.227\dB$ for the fixed-offset
arm and $+0.697\dB$ for the NMS-off arm --- so contrasts computed under the two aggregations are not
interchangeable. \textbf{Section~\ref{sec:results} remains the primary result and its scoring rule is
unchanged.}

\section{Directions closed before the repair experiment}
\label{app:closed}

Recorded because they explain why other lines were not pursued, not because they bear on the result.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Camera reference-frame dependence.} PRoPE (\arxiv{2507.10496}): ``sensitive to the
arbitrary choice of reference frame, which can hinder generalization.'' Independently, the selector's
own source shows \code{sorted\_frames[0]} is appended before the threshold loop and never reads the
threshold, and slot 0 was identical in 14 of 14 windows, so the state leak provably cannot move the
ray-reference camera. The direction was dead on both counts.
\item \textbf{Evaluation state and resetting.} TTAB (\arxiv{2306.03536}, ICML 2023) distinguishes
episodic adaptation from accumulated state. State isolation therefore remains a necessary control in
this work, not a contribution.
\item \textbf{Source-conflict and future-geometry selection candidates} (project-internal,
\code{SOCF-A}, \code{FGB-SI}) were stopped before execution: they proposed selection policies before
anything was established about what the consumer responds to.
\end{itemize}
