## 3. The convergent candidate method

Three independent sources (codex R34, and two GPT-6 Pro answers) converged on one direction:

> **Predict the geometry of surfaces that are not currently visible, before they are revealed, and
> condition the generator on that prediction.**

Names in the sources: CAGF (context-to-target geometry/appearance field, R34), BLSC (boundary-linked
scene completion, Pro answer 1), Reveal-Event World Modeling (Pro answer 2).

Common core:
- From only the 4 delivered context frames, infer hidden-surface geometry and appearance
  (e.g. which visible surface fragments continue behind an occluder, and when they become visible).
- Render that prediction into the target cameras and inject it through a zero-initialised adapter
  in the VMem denoiser; train the adapter and denoiser LoRA (authorised: F).
- Never write predicted geometry into the measured-evidence memory.

### R37 hostile-prior-art narrowing (2026-09-23)

The broad wording above is no longer sufficient for an innovation claim. Recent hostile review finds
that generic 3D belief maintenance, unseen-region completion, uncertainty-guided novel-view synthesis,
occlusion/re-entry continuity, and fixed-lag smoothing are already occupied. The surviving formulation
is narrower:

> A camera-conditioned generative 3D world model keeps an auditable predictive belief for a hidden
> surface; a later contradictory reveal performs a local posterior correction on that same belief, and
> a future novel view benefits while an untouched region is preserved.

This is a candidate information pathway, not a validated method. It must be separated from forward
out-of-sight evolution, append-only retrieval, generic completion, and SLAM pose/map smoothing. The
minimum pilot requires hidden → delayed reveal → reappearance episodes, before/after belief error,
local correction, untouched-region preservation, and capacity-matched append-only and generic-completion
controls. The full hostile prior-art decision is recorded in
`work/agents/CODEX_R37_INNOVATION_DECISION_20260923.md`.

R38-R40 further narrowed the candidate after checking WRBench, GEM-Occ, residual-transfer, Edicho,
and counterfactual-world-modeling prior art. The working label is **CGLR (Conservative Geometric
Local Revision)**, with **CRR (Causal Reveal Residual)** as the only candidate mechanism. RRST
(Reveal-Residual Surface Transport) is merely an engineering substrate because residual transport is
already published. The surviving hypothesis is: a real delayed RGB-D reveal triggers a support-local
change in the same hidden-surface belief, and that change affects at least two future-camera
generations while no-reveal and outside-support branches remain unchanged. It must beat append-only retrieval,
equal-capacity generic completion, global update, correspondence-guided editing, selective
occlusion-update controls, and no-transport controls. The only remaining distinction is the real
delayed contradictory RGB-D event reused across multiple future camera-conditioned views under a
same-noise third-camera test. See `work/agents/CODEX_R40_HOSTILE_UPDATE_20260923.md` and
`work/agents/CODEX_R39_INNOVATION_SYNTHESIS_20260923.md`.

R41-R42 pivot the ranking. **RCA (Revealed-Contradiction Attribution)** is now the primary candidate:
it routes a delayed residual among scene-surface, camera-gauge, and transient causes before any
hidden-belief revision, directly testing the C8 slot-0 confound. **BRD (Bidirectional Reveal Deletion)**
is secondary: negative reveal evidence deletes predicted ghost surfaces. CRR is retained as a causal
evaluation operator, not a standalone novelty axis. Both RCA and BRD are CPU-contract hypotheses and
must beat robust pose/update, occupancy, append-only, and generic-completion controls. See
`work/agents/CODEX_R42_INNOVATION_DECISION_20260923.md`.

If both mechanisms collapse into prior art, the fallback is DCR, a static-geometry delayed
contradictory RGB-D reveal setting with positive/negative reveals, two future-camera queries, and
strict support/locality/anti-leakage scoring. It must be separated from dynamic hide-and-return
benchmarks and object-permanence belief models.

What must be shown for it to count (all three sources insist on the same control):
- It beats a **capacity-matched generic completion / depth-conditioned adapter trained on the same
  data**. Otherwise the gain is just "more geometry input" or "more parameters".
- The gain must appear in **generated RGB geometry** (depth recovered from generated images vs
  reference RGB-D), concentrated on newly revealed regions, not only in an auxiliary head.
- Evaluation on independent held-out scenes (ScanNet++ application still to be started).

Fit with the original proposal: this is the proposal's "persistent geometric context /
geometry-conditioned generation" with "lightweight geometry-aware adaptation".
